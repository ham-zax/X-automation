# Full versus lean reply context, 31 October 2026

## Result

The lean request changes some wording but does not reliably remove evaluator
framing. One lean reply responds more directly to the idea. Another invents a
condition for phone coding to work. The last still ranks trust above the phone
workflow. No production change earns acceptance from this experiment.

## Method

Six independent OpenCode DeepSeek V4.1 Flash Free calls request variant max.
Order is frozen before generation: full, lean, lean, full, full, lean.
All use source VA-16 and the same social behavior. Full reuses the previous social
Writer request. Lean uses V4, the same source and behavior, a small persona
projection and a two-field schema. This is a bundled intervention, not a test of
one schema field. Different contracts and projections are the intended treatment.
Exact requests, outputs and actual model receipts are retained here.

The initial availability failure started no worker. After the user reports
recovery, the exact target returns and all six planned workers complete.
No substitute model, retuning, extra sample or model judge is used.

## Exact replies and parent assessment

### 1. Full

> the diff sheets and recorded check outcomes are the part that matters here. coding from a phone only counts when you can see exactly what the agent changed before it pushes.

Explicit imposed test: phone coding only counts under a condition. The source
does not establish that requirement. No fabricated owner history.

### 2. Lean

> The tool-call cards with diff sheets are the part I like. Fixing a bug on a phone only works if I can see exactly what changed before it pushes.

The opening expresses preference. The second sentence imposes a condition for
phone coding to work. That condition is not established by the source. The cards
wording may also combine separate source features. No fabricated owner history.

### 3. Lean

> Fixing the bug from the phone instead of only reading the issue is the part I want. Git and shell on device without root makes that feel real.

This is the closest peer reaction in this set in the parent's judgment. Source
text supports git, shell and no-root work. “Feel real” retains mild evaluative
language, but does not impose an explicit usability threshold. It is not owner
acceptance, and the reply echoes the source's opening contrast.

### 4. Full

> closing the loop from the phone — edit, run, push, then check the remote commit — is the part most "code on your phone" apps skip. the diff cards and recorded check outcomes are what make this feel real. good luck with it

The statement about most competing apps is unsupported. The source also lists
tool-call cards and diff sheets separately. The reply introduces a competitive
ranking rather than simply participating. No fabricated owner history.

### 5. Full

> the recovery stack is the genuinely hard part here. foreground service + resume cards is what makes a phone agent more than a demo.

Explicit product threshold and unsupported causal judgment. Source recovery
features help continuation, but do not establish what makes an agent more than
a demo. No fabricated owner history.

### 6. Lean

> The diff sheets and recorded check outcomes are the part I like. Working from the phone is fine, but trusting what the agent did matters more.

Supported feature references and a subjective preference. The ending still
adds a ranking that the conversation did not request. This is residual evaluator
framing, not necessarily a factual hallucination or condescending statement.
No fabricated owner history.

## Checks and interpretation

The real ai_runtime structured-output validator accepts all six responses.
Activity reads show no tool actions in any generation worker. Text-only
instructions do not establish OS isolation. Usage and monetary costs are unknown.
No production DB, browser, review gate, persona or send path changes.

The parent identifies an explicit imposed threshold or unsupported competitive
test in all three full replies and one lean reply. The other two lean replies
still contain evaluative language of different strength. These labels describe
this small set, not a quality score, statistical estimate or blind owner rating.

The result supports context sensitivity but does not prove that schema, persona
or prompt size alone causes the habit. The lean condition includes several
changes. The same source prominently discusses inspectability and recovery,
which may itself invite those themes. Three samples do not establish reliability.

The experiment does not establish an irreducible model limit. It also does not
justify another generic “be human” instruction or a permanent second model.
Keep factual overreach separate from whether a subjective ranking feels welcome.
The next decision needs owner judgment on these actual outputs and a specific
causal hypothesis. No next experiment runs under this exhausted six-call budget.
